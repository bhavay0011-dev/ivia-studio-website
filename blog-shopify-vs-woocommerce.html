Shopify vs WooCommerce: how to choose for your online store

We build stores on both, and the honest answer is that neither is better. They suit different businesses. Here is how we help clients decide.

The short version

  • Choose Shopify if you want to focus on selling, not on running a website, and you are happy to work within Shopify's way of doing things.
  • Choose WooCommerce if you need full control, unusual product logic, or you already run a WordPress site with content that ranks.

Shopify: managed and predictable

Shopify is a hosted platform. Hosting, security updates, uptime and the checkout are Shopify's responsibility. That is its biggest strength.

  • Setup is fast and the admin is easy for non-technical teams.
  • Checkout is excellent and well tested, which matters for conversion.
  • Indian payments work through gateways such as Razorpay, Cashfree and PayU, plus cash on delivery.
  • Trade-offs: a monthly plan, transaction fees if you do not use Shopify's own payments where available, and many features arrive as paid apps. Deep checkout customisation is limited on lower plans.

WooCommerce: flexible and yours

WooCommerce is a free plugin for WordPress. You host it yourself, which means you control everything, and you are also responsible for everything.

  • Total control over product types, pricing rules, B2B logic, checkout fields and integrations.
  • No platform transaction fee, only your payment gateway's charges.
  • Strong for content-led SEO, because WordPress is a mature blogging platform.
  • Trade-offs: you need good hosting, regular updates, backups and security. Too many plugins make it slow. Without maintenance, it degrades.

Side by side

  • Who maintains it: Shopify maintains itself. WooCommerce needs someone to update and monitor it.
  • Customisation: Shopify is customisable within limits. WooCommerce can do almost anything with code.
  • Running costs: Shopify is a predictable subscription plus apps. WooCommerce is hosting plus any paid plugins plus maintenance time.
  • SEO: both can rank well. Shopify has a few fixed URL patterns; WooCommerce gives full control.
  • Shipping in India: both connect to Shiprocket and most courier aggregators.
  • GST invoices: both support them, usually through an app or plugin.
A useful test: if you cannot name who will update the store's plugins every month, pick Shopify. If your catalogue or pricing does not fit a standard store, pick WooCommerce.

Which we recommend for whom

  • D2C brands launching quickly: usually Shopify.
  • B2B, wholesale or dealer pricing: often WooCommerce, or Shopify on a higher plan.
  • Content-heavy brands that rank through blogs: often WooCommerce.
  • Teams with no technical person: Shopify.

Whichever you choose, the store should be fast, mobile-first and SEO-friendly from day one. The platform matters less than how well it is built.

Key points

  • Shopify is managed and predictable; WooCommerce is flexible and needs maintenance.
  • Both support Indian payment gateways, COD, Shiprocket and GST invoices.
  • Pick Shopify if nobody will maintain plugins; pick WooCommerce for unusual logic or content-led SEO.
  • How well the store is built matters more than the platform.
