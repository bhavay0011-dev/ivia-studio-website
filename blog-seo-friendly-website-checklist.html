The SEO-friendly website checklist we use before every launch

Most websites do not fail at SEO because of keywords. They fail because Google cannot crawl them properly, they load slowly on a phone, or every page has the same title. This is the checklist we run before any site we build goes live.

1. Google can find and crawl every page

  • robots.txt does not block the site. Staging sites often ship with a "disallow everything" rule that nobody removes.
  • No stray noindex tags. Check the page source for noindex on pages you want ranked.
  • A sitemap.xml lists every real page, and only real pages: no thank-you pages, test pages or duplicates.
  • Internal links reach every page. If a page is only reachable through a form or a script, Google may never find it.

2. Each page has one clear job

  • A unique title tag of roughly 50 to 60 characters that says what the page offers and, if relevant, where.
  • A unique meta description that reads like an ad. It does not rank you directly, but it changes who clicks.
  • Exactly one H1, followed by H2s and H3s in a logical order. Headings are an outline, not a font size.
  • A canonical tag pointing to the preferred URL, so /page, /page/ and /page?ref=x are not treated as three pages.

3. It is fast on a real phone

Google ranks the mobile version of your site. Test on a mid-range phone on mobile data, not on your office Wi-Fi.

  • Images are compressed, sized for the screen and served as WebP or AVIF.
  • Images below the fold load lazily; the main hero image does not.
  • Fonts are limited to the weights you actually use.
  • Third-party scripts (chat widgets, pixels, heatmaps) are audited. Each one costs load time.
  • Core Web Vitals in PageSpeed Insights are green or close to it.

4. Google understands what you are

  • Schema markup for your organisation, services, products, FAQs and breadcrumbs, written as JSON-LD.
  • Consistent name, address and phone across the site, your Google Business Profile and directories.
  • Alt text on images that describes the image, not a list of keywords.

5. The basics that are easy to forget

  • HTTPS everywhere, with HTTP redirecting to HTTPS.
  • A useful 404 page that returns a real 404 status.
  • Old URLs redirected with 301s if you are replacing an existing site. This is where most redesigns lose their rankings.
  • Open Graph tags so links look good when shared on WhatsApp and social media.
  • Google Search Console verified and the sitemap submitted on launch day.
The one that matters most: content written for a real search. A page called "Services" with three vague paragraphs will not rank. A page that answers exactly what someone types into Google, in more detail than anyone else, usually will.

What this checklist will not do

Technical SEO makes you eligible to rank. It does not guarantee position one. Competitive terms still need useful content, links from other sites, reviews and time. But without the basics above, none of that effort pays off.

Key points

  • Make sure Google can crawl every page: robots.txt, sitemap and internal links.
  • Give every page a unique title, description, one H1 and a canonical URL.
  • Test speed on a real phone and fix images, fonts and scripts.
  • Add schema markup and keep your business details consistent.
  • Redirect old URLs when you redesign, and submit your sitemap on launch day.
